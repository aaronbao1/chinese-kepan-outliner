# Divergences from the Tibetan translation workflow

This project parallels Kurt Keutzer's agentic translation workflow for Tibetan Buddhist texts. That workflow gathers a text and its editions and commentaries, makes an *input-text*, and outlines it. The outline is an *independent outline*, which is plugged back into the text as an *outlined-text*. The workflow then segments sentences, cuts the outlined-text into chunks of about four to six sentences (the *outlined-and-chunked-text*), gathers context for each chunk and translates chunk by chunk. The terms are defined in [`glossary.md`](glossary.md).

This repository builds the Chinese counterpart of the steps up to chunking (the `ingest`, `outline`, `segment` and `chunk` stages), plus an `export` stage and an `eval` stage. The `context` and `translate` stages are stubs ([`../pipeline/README.md`](../pipeline/README.md)).

The rule for this document: follow the Tibetan workflow's structure (phases, artefact names, the chunk bound) wherever it is specified, diverge only for a Chinese-specific reason, and record every divergence here. The table below is the authoritative list. Its codes D1–D17, D20 and D21 are cited across the repository; D18 and D19 were proposed for the translation stages in a development plan and are not used here. The step numbers in the table (§1e1 and the like) are section numbers of the Tibetan workflow's manuals, which are not included in this copy.

## Why the Chinese side differs

Two properties of the kēpàn (科判) drive most of the divergences:

- **For a sūtra, the outline is stated in a commentary, not in the sūtra.** The three parts 序分 / 正宗分 / 流通分 and the finer divisions below them are a commentator's analysis of the root text. Different commentators divide the same sūtra differently, so an outline exists per (text, scheme). The Tibetan workflow's worked example is a treatise, outlined from its own text; we take that outline to be the author's.
- **Chinese commentaries and treatises announce their own divisions in the running text** (分二, 分三; 初…次…後…; 初中…). Their outline can often be read from the text by rules.

So the outliner has two modes. In *sūtra mode* it reads a designated commentary and divides the sūtra with it. In *self-outlining mode* it reads a text that announces its own divisions. Both modes produce the same artefacts.

## How to read the table

- **Workflow step** names the step, with its section number in Kurt Keutzer's workflow documents: §0–§2d are steps of the agentic Tibetan model, and §3a and §3b2a1 are steps of the human-translation model it builds on. Those documents are not part of this repository.
- **Kind:**
  - *extension*: the workflow's structure is kept and something is added;
  - *replacement*: a step is done differently;
  - *reordering*: a step moves;
  - *refinement*: the workflow's own idea is carried further;
  - *addition*: a step the workflow does not have.
- **Status** says what this repository has built.
- **Evidence.** Bare research codes such as R03 F22 refer to research notes in the development repository, which are not included in this copy. E00 is the kēpàn census in [`../experiments/E00-kepan-census/`](../experiments/E00-kepan-census/SUMMARY.md).

## D15 in detail: the Chinese extension of the outline schema

The profile is the file `context/baseline/outline-schema-zh.json`: JSON Schema 2020-12, standalone. The base schema `context/baseline/outline-schema.json` is not edited.

- **Opt-in.** A document opts in by setting `metadata.outline_profile = "zh-kepan"`. Without the opt-in, every document valid under the base schema is valid here too; the synthetic `tests/fixtures/outline-base/minimal-base.json` passes both. The opt-in makes the fields below mandatory. Nullable fields must still be present, so every key is on every node.
- **What the validator adds.** `scripts/validate_outline.py` checks what the schema cannot express:
  - unique ids, pre-order, and agreement of parent, level and path;
  - `sibling_index` and the metadata counts;
  - span order within a node, between consecutive siblings of one scheme, and between parent and child;
  - text-id prefixes, and the sūtra-mode rules keyed on `outline_mode`.

  It reports ill-typed input as schema errors instead of crashing.
- **Tests.** `tests/unit/test_outline_schema.py`, with a synthetic zh-kepan example at `tests/fixtures/outline-zh/minimal.json`.
